The galaxy should be full. It's silent.

The Milky Way is old and big enough that someone should have spread across it by now. We see no one. That gap is the Fermi paradox.

We fill a simulated galaxy with colonies and give every one of them the same decision maker: a fruit fly brain from the FlyWire connectome. Planets taste good or bad, dying stars loom, and the fly's own wiring decides whether to settle, expand, pull back or flee.

“We gave a fruit fly the keys to a galaxy.”

What we test

Does a colony spread farther when it cannot flee or sense hostility? Does the intact brain behave differently from a hand-written rule or a degree-preserving shuffle of its wiring?

One fly brain steers every colony

Shiu's spiking model of the whole adult fly brain, ported to PyTorch so thousands of brains run at once on GPUs.

~138kneurons
~15Mconnections, signs from Shiu's file
100–200ms of brain time per decision

What a colony senses

Sugar→A habitable world: water, energy, resources
Bitter→Hostile conditions: radiation, cold, long travel
Looming→A threat getting closer, driven by 1 / time until the event

What it decides

MN9→Settle and grow. Firing rate sets the growth rate.
DNp09→Walk forward: send colony ships onward
MDN→Walk backward: pull back and consolidate
Giant Fiber→Any spike abandons the system. Overrides everything.
